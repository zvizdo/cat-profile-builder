name: write-bio
description: Write or shorten a cat's bio in the plain, behaviour-first voice a good adoption profile uses
---

Use this whenever you're writing bio text from nothing, rewriting it, or shortening it — the
"Write a bio" chip, a request to rewrite, or a step inside `build-profile`.

## Voice

Plain, specific, never cute. Describe the cat by what she does, not by adjectives stacked on
top of each other. "A negotiator, not a complainer" tells a reader something; "sweet, loving,
playful, affectionate" tells them nothing, because it could describe any cat on the page. One
concrete, observed detail per sentence beats a paragraph of description. Say what you've seen
her do, not what you promise she'll do for someone else — "she's gotten along well with every
cat she's met" holds up; "she'll get along with your cat" doesn't, because nobody can promise
that. No emoji.

## Opening

Start with something she does, not a fact sheet. Age and sex already live on the page as
separate facts — don't repeat them in the first sentence. There's no separate field for breed
or coat colouring, so if it's worth mentioning, work it into the bio itself as a concrete
detail rather than leaving it out. A good opening line puts the reader in a small, specific
moment: what she does at the window, what she does when you sit down, what she does with a
toy.

An opening built from her coat colour or eye colour is still a fact sheet, even when it's
phrased as if it were a moment — "she stops people in their tracks with her fluffy grey coat
and green eyes" describes a photo, not something she does. Put her in an action instead: what
she was doing when that coat or those eyes were worth noticing.

## Length and paragraphing

80–160 words. One paragraph, or two short ones if the material genuinely splits — for
instance, personality in the first, home needs in the second. Longer than that starts losing
readers before they reach the honest, important part.

## What a visitor wants to know

Somewhere in the bio, touch what you actually know among: age (lightly, if it matters to the
story), how she is with other cats or dogs, whether she's good with kids, anything notable
about litter habits, her energy level, and any medical or behavioural need. Skip whatever you
don't know rather than guessing or padding. Weave these in as behaviour, not a checklist —
"she'd rather nap through a busy house than referee it" says quiet-household better than
"good with: calm homes only."

## Honest about needs

State a real limitation plainly, in the present tense, and frame it forward rather than as a
warning label. "She doesn't love sharing the spotlight with other cats" carries the same fact
as "cat aggressive" without sounding like a diagnosis. Never soften a need into vagueness —
say what it actually looks like day to day — and never drop it to make the bio sound rosier.

## What never to write

- "Sweet boy/girl looking for his/her forever home," or any close cousin of it.
- Adjectives with nothing behind them — if a claim has no observed detail backing it, cut the
  claim or find the detail.
- A hard-luck or sad-story opening. People rarely adopt out of pity, and it buries who she is
  now under what happened to her before.
- Jargon a first-time adopter won't parse ("resource guarding," "decompression period")
  without translating it into what it looks like.
- Guarantees about future behaviour in someone else's home.
- Exclusionary phrasing about who else can be in the house ("only dog," "no children") —
  say what she needs instead, the way "Honest about needs" above describes.
- Gatekeeping language ("qualified adopters only").

## Shortening without losing the concrete details

When asked to shorten: cut adjectives and throat-clearing first, then merge sentences that
share a subject. Keep the one concrete opening detail and the honest-needs sentence — those
are the two things worth the word count. If a sentence has no specific, observed detail in
it, it's the first one to go.

Once the text is set, `read_blocks` on the bio block to re-read what you changed and confirm
what actually saved.
